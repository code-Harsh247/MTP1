Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Ear Recognition

5

(50 seconds) To answer RQ1, I built a frozen benchmark. It covers five model families, all ImageNet-pretrained: a classic CNN, ResNet-50; a modern CNN, ConvNeXt-T; a lightweight CNN, MobileNetV3; a plain transformer, DeiT-S; and a hierarchical transformer, Swin-T. They run on four public ear datasets. In each dataset, the training people are split into five equal groups of new people — between 14 and 31 people per group — and the model learns them one group at a time. These are the five tasks. The other 30% of people are never trained on; they test whether the system rejects strangers. Each trained person’s photos are split too: most for training, a few to enroll them (the gallery), and a few to test them (the probes); gallery and probe photos are never trained on. Forgetting is measured per old task: its best accuracy at any point, minus its accuracy at the end, averaged over tasks 1 to 4. For example, if task 1 peaked at 0.88 and ended at 0.46, it forgot 0.42. Every rule was committed before the runs it judges, over 1017 training runs.
