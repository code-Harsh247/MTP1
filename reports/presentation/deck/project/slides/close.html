Contributions, limits and next steps

Contributions

  • A frozen continual ear recognition benchmark: 4 datasets × 5 families
  • Evidence that forgetting is real and model-independent on large data
  • The first test of NL’s CMS on vision and biometrics
  • Diagnostics that locate why it failed

Limitations

  • The UERC dataset is not included
  • Three seeds on EarVN; tiny task tests on AMI and AWE
  • R4 to R5b are exploratory, designed after R3
  • The dilution tests use an oracle that protects only task 1

Next steps

  • Extend CMS to the neck, attention and normalisation layers
  • Select or gate the copies per input instead of blending them
  • Combine CMS with LwF, for enrollment without stored images

Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Ear Recognition

(60 seconds) To close. The contributions: a frozen benchmark for continual ear recognition across four datasets and five model families; evidence that forgetting is real and does not depend on the architecture; the first test of Nested Learning’s CMS on vision and biometrics; and diagnostics that locate why it failed. The limitations: the largest dataset, UERC, is not included; EarVN has three seeds and the small datasets have tiny test sets; the follow-up stages are exploratory; and the dilution tests use an oracle. The next steps follow directly from the findings: extend CMS beyond the pointwise layers, choose between copies instead of blending them, and combine CMS with LwF so new people can be enrolled without storing anyone’s images.
