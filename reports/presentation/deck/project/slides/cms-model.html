(50 seconds) This is the model I tested. On top it is the same pipeline as before — image, backbone, neck, ArcFace head — with one change inside the backbone. The dashed panel zooms into one backbone block. The rest of the block — attention or the spatial convolutions, and the norms — stays as it was and trains normally. But the pointwise layer, the one that plays the role of a Transformer’s MLP, is replaced by a CMS layer: three copies of that pretrained layer, updated every step, every 16 steps and every 256 steps. Their outputs are combined by a learned weighted sum. The mixing weights come from a softmax of logits that start at zero, so each copy gets one third, and since all copies equal the pretrained layer, the CMS model starts out computing exactly the pretrained model. The row at the bottom shows how many layers this replaces in each family: from 12 in the transformers to 32 in ResNet.
